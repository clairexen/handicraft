## SANE: Self-And-Next Encoder Transformers for Latent Think-Ahead Generation

### Self-And-Next Encoding

This proposal introduces **SANE (Self-And-Next Encoder)** transformers, a
looping transformer architecture that constructs and iteratively refines a
short latent continuation of future tokens. The model state is divided into two
interleaved streams within the hidden dimension: the **self stream** (even
dimensions), representing the current position, and the **next stream** (odd
dimensions), representing a prediction of the following position. Token
embeddings are injected only into the self stream, while the next stream is
initialized to zero. After each transformer pass the model produces a refined
self representation and a next representation that acts as a latent proposal
for the subsequent position.

The transformer adds function outputs to its state vector twice per layer: at
the end of attention and then again at the end of the MLP. For each of these
two points in each layer we learn an "alpha" vector of per-self-feature scalars
and a "beta" vector of per-next-feature scalars.

Whenever we update

	state_at_pos(t) += update_t

we also perform

	self(state_at_pos(t+1)) += alpha .* next(update_t)     ... in "decode" or "encode" mode
	next(state_at_pos(t-1)) += beta .* self(update_t)      ... in "reverse" or "encode" mode

These SANE alpha (forward in time) and beta (backwards in time) channels create
semantically meaningful high-bandwith pathways between positions. The alpha
channel enables the "next" stream at position t to steer the "self" stream at
position t+1, and the beta channel enables the "self" stream at position t to
steer the "next" stream at position t-1.

For clarity: when `Z=1` (plain decoder spans) the MTLD grid acts like a decoder
when we look at how *spans* interact. Each span may only attend to earlier
spans, and only the alpha channel delivers information from one span into the
next. Once `Z>1` the columns inside each span behave like an encoder strip (the
later `k_1 < k_2` / `z_1=0` rules describe this regime). Every column can
attend to every other column in the same span (regardless of direction), and
both
alpha and beta propagation operate so features can shuttle forward or backward
across the offsets. This reflects the desire to let the latent future positions
collaborate freely without compromising the overall left-to-right
autoregressive discipline across spans.

The alpha and beta couplings are meaningful for any grid regime (decoder `D`,
encoder `E`, or reverse `R`). However, the concrete training loop described in
this document is intrinsically left-to-right, so the current proof-of-concept
focuses on `D` grids. Implementations should therefore reject `E` or `R` grids
whenever they request multi-token lookahead (`X>1` or `Z>1`).

(For a first proof-of-concept implementation in grce.py we only implement the
SANE alpha and beta channels within a `D` "Grid".)


### Multi-Token Looped Decoder (MTLD)

Instead of simply predicting the token t+1 from token t, the MTLD uses an
encoder-like Z positions wide block of parallel Y-looping transformers
(connected via unmasked attention and SANE alpha and beta channels) to create
an internal representation of the next Z-1 tokens (t+1 ... t+Z-1), modelling
and predicting a space of possible continuation sequences of length Z-1. During
the initial `x=0` pass we still feed only the *current* token t into the first
column at `z=0`; the remaining columns are seeded with [|find-self|] so they
must infer their respective futures internally. Token t+1 is therefore
predicted twice at this stage: the decoder column at `z=0` produces it on its
"next" stream, and the encoder-style column at `z=1` emits the same token on
its "self" stream so downstream logic can reuse the latent future state without
swapping parity lanes.

The SANE alpha and beta channels create high bandwidth channels between
neighbouring positions, and the "sliding window" approach described below
preserves most of the internal state from one token to the next.

The hope is that this architecture will enable smaller transformers to think
ahead, without having to create, store, and manipulate a deep and complex model
of the immediate future within a single transformer instance.

Note on looping: We pass the transformer state vector through
RMSNorm(elementwise_affine=False) at the end of each loop iteration. (The
`loop_ln` block inside grce.py is reserved for the traditional X-axis
"thinking" passes in forward (`F`) rows and should not activate during SANE
spans; in the SANE path we apply the RMSNorm-style normalization described here
instead.)


#### Mode of operation / inference

We operate on an encoder-like block of Z parallel Y-looping transforers. The
looping transformer at position z=0 is fed the previous token (at index t) and
the [|predict-next|] embedding on the "self" stream, and after Y loop
iterations we obtain the token t+1 from the "next" output stream at position
z=0. The inputs to the looping transformers at positions z>0 are fed the
[|find-self|] embedding in the "self" stream.

After predicting a token in this way, we do not throw away the entire state!
Instead we shift the transformer state one position to the left, shifting away
the previous first position, that just predicted a token, and creating a new
most-right last position (initialized with [|find-self|] in the "self" stream).
We then (re-)add the embedding for the emitted token and [|predict-next|] to
the new first position. Then we loop Y times on that new configuration, and so
forth.


#### Training

In training we emulate that sliding window behavior without a sliding window.
Instead of moving a fixed-size active zone forward and shifting states one
position to the left, as in inference, we simply treat the column with z=x as
the new *leading position* of the active zone and **add** (not replace) its
true self token plus [|predict-next|] into that column. Column z=x-1, no longer
part of the active zone, is kept unmodified and constrained in the same way,
doing another Y loops (and providing K/V for its position to the active-zone
columns).

During the pass with index x the active zone therefore consists of all columns
with z>=x, just like inference where everything to the right of the decoder
head keeps thinking ahead. All earlier columns (z<x) stay frozen: we do not
inject new embeddings or flip their control mode, and their supervision target
remains whatever it was in the preceding pass. They merely keep looping so
their cached K/V continues to be available to the currently active column. In
lowercase `z` layouts those frozen columns can still leak in their targets via
beta propagation and non-causal attention, whereas uppercase `Z` layouts block
those retrograde paths so the frozen losses stay roughly flat. Only the column
currently leading the active zone (z=x) receives an additive injection of its true self
token and [|predict-next|], then gets supervised to emit t+z+1 on its next
stream. No column is reinitialized during an inter-X transition—we always
preserve the accumulated hidden state and only add new signals on top.

Columns farther to the right but still inside the active zone (z>x) retain
their previous speculative role. They keep their [|find-self|] inputs, remain
detached from the newly leading column of the active zone, and are constrained to repeat the same
t+z self targets as before. Each inter-X transition therefore promotes exactly
one column while everyone else keeps looping in place, approximating the
sliding-window behavior we want at inference time (the only difference is that
the training window gradually shrinks because we cannot append a fresh
rightmost column).

We are constuctring a "Grid" (in notex.txt/grce.py lingo) to train and evaluate
"SANE" thinking within `D` regime of th grid concept specifically
(left-to-right-decoder without GRCE/XCTX recurrent context).

Let's call `x in [0...X-1]` the X-axis, the "time-axis", indexed by the token index `x`.
Let's call `y in [0...Y-1]` the Y-axis, the "loop-axis", indexed by the loop iteration `y`.
Let's further call `z in [0...Z-1]` the Z-axis, the "depth" of the current thought, indexed by the token offset `z`.

An MTLD with X=1 and Z=1 is simply a regular single-token Y-looped decoder.
Thus the grid layout language `64D4Y` is identical to `64D1X4Y1Z`. Let further
be `t in [0..T-1]` the (input) token axis. The numner T=64 in `64D4Y` denotes
the number of input tokens.

We maintain spans of Z=4 columns but truncate the last such span so it only
contains the first column (z=0). This guarantees that the last column in our
grid has the properties we'd expect of the final decoder column and avoids
ragged tensor shapes near the boundary. Let's use N to capture the number of
columns in our grid. Then N=(T-1)*Z+1. For the concrete walkthrough below we
choose `T=65`, i.e. layout `65D 3X 2Y 4Z`, which yields N=(65-1)*4+1=257. Thus
the column index `i in [0...N-1]` can be written as `i = t*Z+z`.

So we have 64 full-width encoder-style spans indexed by `k in [0...K-1]`, each
4 columns wide, spanning a total of 64*4=256 columns, plus a single regular
decoder column in a trimmed last span at span index `k=K`.

Because we have X=3 and Y=2 in our example we have a total of X*Y=6 total loop
iteratrions during training. Inference does not have a direct analogue to X
because generation can, in principle, continue indefinitely; X simply captures
how many autoregressive steps we choose to unroll while training. Y-looping is
trivial so we only detail the three X-loops here. Let's start with x=0:

On every z=0 position we initialize the transformer state with the embedding
for the input token `t` and the [|predict-next|] embedding in the "self" stream
at the position `i`, and we constrain the output at position `i` to contain the
embedding of the token `t+1` in the "next" stream output. During this first
`x=0` pass the other positions `z>0` receive only the [|find-self|] encoding in
their "self" stream; after looping they are constrained to emit token `t+z` on
their self stream, so `z=1` redundantly carries token `t+1` alongside the `z=0`
next-stream prediction.

After Y=2 iterations we enter the `x=1` region. Only the column with `z=x`
receives another additive injection of its token embedding `t+z` plus
[|predict-next|] before being constrained, after Y=2 more iterations, to emit
`t+z+1` on its "next" stream. All columns with `z<x` remain untouched (keep
looping under their previous supervision), and the `z>x` positions likewise
retain their prior [|find-self|] inputs and self targets. Each X-transition
therefore promotes exactly one column, adds the relevant embeddings on top of
its existing state, and leaves every other column alone.

With regards to attention a transformer with a position with k_2 and z_2
attents to all other transformers at k_1 and z_1 when either `k_1 < k_2 and
z_1=0` or `k_1 == k_2`.

With regards to SANE alpha and beta channels there are two aspects. Within a
goup (k_1 == k_2) there's alpha propagation from each z_1 to z_2 and beta
propagation from each z_2 to z_1 where z_1+1 == z_2. From one group to the next
(k_1+1 == k_2) there's alpha propagation from z_1=0 in k_1 to z_2=0 in k_2.

### Lowercase vs uppercase Z modifiers

We expose two layout suffix families for multi-token spans. Lowercase modifiers
(`Nz`) behave exactly as described above: every column within a span attends to
every other column in that span, regardless of `z`, and both alpha and beta
propagation flow freely so latent futures can steer earlier offsets. Uppercase
modifiers (`NZ`) build the same Z-wide grid but restrict those backward paths to
the *active zone* (`z >= x`). Columns that have already left the active zone are
masked out of the non-causal attention pattern and no longer accept or emit
beta updates, so they behave exactly like causal decoder positions once their
token has been predicted. This allows experiments that deliberately permit
ground-truth leakage (lowercase `z`) and tighter causal experiments (uppercase
`Z`) to coexist within the same layout language.

### Explicit Rules for Attention and Alpha/Beta Steering

In all of the following cases, i2 is the position receiving information and i1 is
the position sending information.

#### Explicit Rules for Attention and Alpha/Beta Steering in "causal" `Z` Mode

In "causal" (`Z`) mode a position t2, x2, y2, z2, i2=t2*Z+z2
attents to a position t1, x1, y1, z1, i1=t1*Z+z1
exactly iff:

	(t1 < t2 AND z1=0) OR (t1 = t2 AND z1 <= z2) OR (t1 = t2 AND z1 >= x1 AND z2 >= x2)

In "causal" (`Z`) mode a position t2, x2, y2, z2, i2=t2*Z+z2
receives alpha-propagation from position t1, x1, y1, z1, i1=t1*Z+z1
exactly iff:

	(t1 = t2 AND z2 = z1+1) OR (t2 = t1+1 AND z1=0 AND z2=0)

In "causal" (`Z`) mode a position t2, x2, y2, z2, i2=t2*Z+z2
receives beta-propagation from position t1, x1, y1, z1, i1=t1*Z+z1
exactly iff:

	(t1 = t2 AND z1 = z2+1 AND z1 >= x1 AND z2 >= x2)

#### Explicit Rules for Attention and Alpha/Beta Steering in "non-causal" `z` Mode

In "non-causal" (`z`) mode a position t2, x2, y2, z2, i2=t2*Z+z2
attents to a position t1, x1, y1, z1, i1=t1*Z+z1
exactly iff:

	(t1 < t2 AND z1=0) OR (t1 = t2)

In "non-causal" (`z`) mode a position t2, x2, y2, z2, i2=t2*Z+z2
receives alpha-propagation from position t1, x1, y1, z1, i1=t1*Z+z1
exactly iff:

	(t1 = t2 AND z2 = z1+1) OR (t2 = t1+1 AND z1=0 AND z2=0)

In "non-causal" (`z`) mode a position t2, x2, y2, z2, i2=t2*Z+z2
receives beta-propagation from position t1, x1, y1, z1, i1=t1*Z+z1
exactly iff:

	(t1 = t2 AND z1 = z2+1)

### Explicit Rules for Input and Supervision

Let `x ∈ [0, X-1]`, `y ∈ [0, Y-1]`, `z ∈ [0, Z-1]`, and `t` denote the token
index such that column `i = t*Z + z`.

**Inputs**

- For all `x, y, z>0`: self(i, x, y) = [|find-self|], next(i, x, y) = 0.
- For `z=0`: self(i, x, y=0) = token(t) + [|predict-next|]; next(i, x, y=0) = 0.
- When a column becomes the leading position of the active zone at pass `x`
  (i.e. the column whose z equals x) we add the true token for its offset and
  [|predict-next|] to its self stream: self(i, x, y=0) += token(t+z) +
  [|predict-next|].
- No embeddings are removed between X-steps; state is carried over and only augmented.

**Supervision** (LM head applied either on self or next stream as specified)

- For `z=0`: supervise next(i, x, y=Y-1) → token(t+1) at every X-step.
- For `0 < z < x`: supervise self(i, x, y=Y-1) → token(t+z) (frozen columns).
- For `z = x`: supervise next(i, x, y=Y-1) → token(t+z+1).
- For `z > x`: supervise self(i, x, y=Y-1) → token(t+z) (speculative columns keep their targets).

----

This encoder-like intra-span coupling is intentionally stronger than what we
ultimately want to ship. When X>0 we can leak ground-truth information
backwards: on the second iteration (x=1) the freshly injected z=1 column can
send its correct self token to z=0 via attention/beta, telling the decoder what
it needs to predict. On x=2 that leakage can travel from z=2 back to z=0, then
through the alpha bridge into the next span (k+1), revealing its upcoming
target as well. The uppercase `Z` modifier provides the tightened behavior that
blocks these retrograde channels as soon as a column leaves the active zone,
while the lowercase `z` modifier keeps the relaxed proof-of-concept wiring so
we can verify the machinery is operating correctly.

So for example --layout '16[ 64E 2Y = 64D 4Y = 64D 3X 2Y 4z >> S$$ ]' should
learn easily how to produce low losses at S10, S20, and S21 because non-causal
attention and beta-propagation within a span provide pathways to access the
token values they are trying to predict. Replacing `4z` with `4Z` blocks those
pathways and eliminates the low-loss columns.

Notes on the $$ syntax: E.g. S20 means x=2, z=0.) The active zone is z>=x. S20
and S21 are not in the active zone. (S22 and S23 are in the active zone.) The
above statements says that the losses from outside of the active zone (S10,
S20, and S21) will learn low losses with `4z` because non-causal attention and
beta-propagation in the non-active positions will provide them with pathways to
access the injected tokens they are trying to predict. Using `4Z` insted of
`4z` will block those pathways.

----

### Elaborated example

Elaborating further on the `3X 2Y 4z >> S$$` example. Loss function is LM head, either on "self" or on "next" stream.

#### Column z=0

The history of the first column (z=0) should look like this:

initialize:
  self(state) = [|predict-next|] + token(t)
  next(state) = 0

Y=2 rounds of transformer with alpha-propagation to x'=x, z'=z+1 and alpha-propagation to x'=x+1, z'=0 (and alpha-propagation from x'=x-1, z'=0 and beta-propagation from x'=x, z'=z+1).

constrain (loss S00):
  next(state) = token(t+1)

Y=2 rounds of transformer with alpha-propagation to x'=x, z'=z+1 and alpha-propagation to x'=x+1, z'=0 (and alpha-propagation from x'=x-1, z'=0 and beta-propagation from x'=x, z'=z+1).

constrain (loss S10):
  next(state) = token(t+1)

Y=2 rounds of transformer with alpha-propagation to x'=x, z'=z+1 and alpha-propagation to x'=x+1, z'=0 (and alpha-propagation from x'=x-1, z'=0 and beta-propagation from x'=x, z'=z+1).

constrain (loss S20):
  next(state) = token(t+1)

#### Column z=1

The history of the second column (z=1) should look like this:

initialize:
  self(state) = [|find-self|]
  next(state) = 0

Y=2 rounds of transformer with alpha-propagation from x'=x, z'=z+1 and to x'=x, z'=z+1 and [...]

constrain (loss S01):
  self(state) = token(t+1)

becomes the leading position of the active zone (x=1):
  self(state) += [|predict-next|] + token(t+1)

Y=2 rounds of transformer with alpha-propagation from x'=x, z'=z+1 and to x'=x, z'=z+1 and [...]

constrain (loss S11):
  next(state) = token(t+2)

Y=2 rounds of transformer with alpha-propagation from x'=x, z'=z+1 and to x'=x, z'=z+1 and [...]

constrain (loss S21):
  next(state) = token(t+2)
