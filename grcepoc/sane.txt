**SANE: Self-And-Next Encoder Transformers for Latent Think-Ahead Generation**

This proposal introduces **SANE (Self-And-Next Encoder)** transformers, a looping transformer architecture that constructs and iteratively refines a short latent continuation of future tokens. The model state is divided into two interleaved streams within the hidden dimension: the **self stream** (even dimensions), representing the current position, and the **next stream** (odd dimensions), representing a prediction of the following position. Token embeddings are injected only into the self stream, while the next stream is initialized to zero. After each transformer pass the model produces a refined self representation and a next representation that acts as a latent proposal for the subsequent position.

Instead of repeatedly refining a single token position, loop iterations expand into a **multi-position encoder span**. The first loop processes the current token at position `t`. In later loops additional positions are introduced so that after `k` iterations the model operates on a span `[t, t+1, …, t+k−1]`. All positions form an encoder-style transformer block and therefore attend to each other, allowing the model to jointly refine representations of the current token and several predicted future tokens.

The **self→next propagation rule** initializes new positions while preserving previously computed structure. When creating position `t+1`, the model does not overwrite its self stream. Instead, the next stream produced at position `t` is **added** to the self representation propagated from the previous loop iteration. This additive update combines two signals: (1) the existing representation of the position carried forward through the loop, and (2) the prediction from the preceding position. The additive formulation preserves information already present in the span while still allowing forward prediction signals to influence downstream positions.

Training uses **think-ahead blocks** of length `1 ≤ H ≤ N`, where `N` is the number of loop iterations specified by the `Ny` modifier in the layout. The suffix `HZ` (or `Hz`, see below) on a segment pinches off a contiguous span of `H` positions within the grid. Only the first token in that span is revealed to the network at loop iteration 1; the remaining `H−1` positions have their self streams zeroed and their decoder logits masked so they behave exactly like length‑1 spans. After each loop iteration the *next* stream from position `i` is **added** to the *self* stream of position `i+1`. Consequently:

* Loop 1 only updates position `t` (span offset 0) and records its next prediction.
* Loop 2 copies that next stream into position `t+1`, allowing the encoder to operate on two positions.
* …
* Loop `H` is the first iteration where all `H` positions participate simultaneously and the span’s final position emits an initial prediction for token `t+H`.
* If `H < N`, the remaining `N−H` loops continue to refine the entire span jointly while the additive self→next propagation keeps flowing across the `H−1` adjacent pairs.

Except for these masks and the inter-loop self→next addition, the transformer runs the same computation as a plain `Ny` segment. The **lower-right triangle** of the span (positions introduced in later loops) still executes the full causal attention stack as if `H=1`; we only zero out their interface to the encoder-style block above/left of the triangle until the loop that activates that position. This preserves “normal” key/value entries for non-leading tokens so later positions can attend to them once they become active.

To stabilize the recurrent structure created by looping and rolling latent continuation, the output of each loop iteration is passed through **RMSNorm with no affine parameters** before being fed into the next loop. This normalization constrains the magnitude of representations traveling through the recurrent pathway and helps prevent drift or amplification across successive iterations.

During inference the model maintains a **rolling latent continuation**. After generating a span `[t, …, t+H−1]`, the next token is emitted from the first position. The latent state is then shifted forward by one position: the embedding of the emitted token initializes the new first position, while the next streams continue to seed subsequent positions through the additive self→next propagation rule. Additional loop iterations refine the updated span `[t+1, …, t+H]`. Because the emitted token is sampled from the same jointly refined state that produced the continuation, the latent future generally remains consistent with the generated output.

For compatibility with standard autoregressive decoding, **only the key/value tensors produced in the final loop iteration**—the one that generates the final prediction—are committed to the attention **KV cache**. Intermediate loop iterations therefore remain internal refinement steps and do not expand the external context memory.

A central motivation for SANE is the creation of a **high-bandwidth latent channel between adjacent positions**. Conventional autoregressive transformers transmit information from one token to the next only through the discrete token itself. In SANE, the next stream provides a direct continuous pathway from one position’s representation to the self stream of the next. This enables internal reasoning state, planning signals, and intermediate computations to propagate forward without needing to be encoded into tokens.

Two layout suffixes control how much of the span’s key/value state is exposed:

* `z` (lowercase) hides the encoder-style K/V tensors generated by the non-leading positions when they wake up. Downstream segments therefore only see standard caches produced by the leading token of the span, matching the behavior of classic decode passes.
* `Z` (uppercase) keeps those K/V entries visible once they exist, allowing later segments to attend directly to the latent predictions.

Both variants share the same masks, span-growth, and self→next add rules described above; they only differ in the attention mask applied to later segments, which determines whether the non-leading positions’ caches become part of the observable context.

The resulting architecture combines transformer attention with a persistent forward-flowing latent state and an iterative refinement mechanism. The model maintains and updates a short internal continuation of upcoming tokens while generating text, producing a rolling planning horizon reminiscent of incremental human language production. By preserving internal computation across token boundaries and allowing future tokens to influence each other before emission, SANE transformers may improve generation stability, local coherence, and the ability to sustain multi-step reasoning.
