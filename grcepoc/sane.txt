## SANE: Self-And-Next Encoder Transformers for Latent Think-Ahead Generation

### Self-And-Next Encoding

This proposal introduces **SANE (Self-And-Next Encoder)** transformers, a looping transformer architecture that constructs and iteratively refines a short latent continuation of future tokens. The model state is divided into two interleaved streams within the hidden dimension: the **self stream** (even dimensions), representing the current position, and the **next stream** (odd dimensions), representing a prediction of the following position. Token embeddings are injected only into the self stream, while the next stream is initialized to zero. After each transformer pass the model produces a refined self representation and a next representation that acts as a latent proposal for the subsequent position.

The transformer adds function outputs to its state vector twice per layer: at the end of attention and then again at the end of the MLP. For each of these two points in each layer we learn an "alpha" vector of per-self-feature scalars and a "beta" vector of per-next-feature scalars.

Whenever we update

	state_at_pos(t) += update_t

we also perform

	self(state_at_pos(t+1)) += alpha .* next(update_t)     ... in "decode" or "encode" mode
	next(state_at_pos(t-1)) += beta .* self(update_t)      ... in "reverse" or "encode" mode

These SANE alpha (forward in time) and beta (backwards in time) channels create semantically meaningful high-bandwith pathways between positions. The alpha channel enables the "next" stream at position t to steer the "self" stream at position t+1, and the beta channel enables the "self" stream at position t to steer the "next" stream at position t-1.

For clarity: the MTLD grid acts like a decoder when we look at how *spans* interact. Each Z-wide span may only attend to earlier spans, and only the alpha channel delivers information from one span into the next. Inside a span, however, the Z positions behave like an encoder strip. Every column can attend to every other column in the same span (regardless of direction), and both alpha and beta propagation operate so features can shuttle forward or backward across the offsets. This reflects the desire to let the latent future positions collaborate freely without compromising the overall left-to-right autoregressive discipline across spans.

The alpha and beta couplings are meaningful for any grid regime (decoder `D`, encoder `E`, or reverse `R`). However, the concrete training loop described in this document is intrinsically left-to-right, so the current proof-of-concept focuses on `D` grids. Implementations should therefore reject `E` or `R` grids whenever they request multi-token lookahead (`X>1` or `Z>1`).

(For a first proof-of-concept implementation in grce.py we only implement the SANE alpha and beta channels within a `D` "Grid".)


### Multi-Token Looped Decoder (MTLD)

Instead of simply predicting the token t+1 from token t, the MTLD uses an encoder-like Z positions wide block of parallel Y-looping transformers (connected via unmasked attention and SANE alpha and beta channels) to create an internal representation of the next Z-1 tokens (t+1 ... t+Z-1), modelling and predicting a space of possible continuation sequences of length Z-1.

The SANE alpha and beta channels create high bandwidth channels between neighbouring positions, and the "sliding window" approach described below preserves most of the internal state from one token to the next.

The hope is that this architecture will enable smaller transformers to think ahead, without having to create, store, and manipulate a deep and complex model of the immediate future within a single transformer instance.

Note on looping: We pass the transformer state vector through RMSNorm(elementwise_affine=False) at the end of each loop iteration.


#### Mode of operation / inference

We operate on an encoder-like block of Z parallel Y-looping transforers. The looping transformer at position z=0 is fed the previous token (at index t) and the [|predict-next|] embedding on the "self" stream, and after Y loop iterations we obtain the token t+1 from the "next" output stream at position z=0. The inputs to the looping transformers at positions z>0 are fed the [|find-self|] embedding in the "self" stream.

After predicting a token in this way, we do not throw away the entire state. Instead we shift the transformer state one position to the left, shifting away the previous firty position, that just predicted a token, and creating a new most-right last position (initialized with [|find-self|] in the "self" stream). We then (re-)add the embedding for the emitted token and [|predict-next|] to the new first position. Then we loop Y times on that new configuration, and so forth.


#### Training

We are constuctring a "Grid" (in notex.txt/grce.py lingo) to train and evaluate "SANE" thinking within `D` regime of th grid concept specifically (left-to-right-decoder without GRCE/XCTX recurrent context).

Let's call `x in [0...X-1]` the X-axis, the "time-axis", indexed by the token index `x`.
Let's call `y in [0...Y-1]` the Y-axis, the "loop-axis", indexed by the loop iteration `y`.
Let's further call `z in [0...Z-1]` the Z-axis, the "depth" of the current thought, indexed by the token offset `z`.

An MTLD with X=1 and Z=1 is simply a regular single-token Y-looped decoder. Thus the grid layout language `64D4Y` is identical to `64D1X4Y1Z`. Let further be `t in [0..T-1]` the (input) token axis. The numner T=64 in `64D4Y` denotes the number of input tokens.

We maintain spans of Z=4 columns but truncate the last such span so it only contains the first column (z=0). This guarantees that the last column in our grid has the properties we'd expect of the final decoder column and avoids ragged tensor shapes near the boundary. Let's use N to capture the number of columns in our grid. Then N=(T-1)*Z+1. Let's use the grid `65D 3X 2Y 4Z` (thus N=64*4+1=257) as an example going forward. Thus the column index `i in [0...N-1]` can be written as `i = t*Z+z`.

So we have 64 full-width encoder-style spans indexed by `k in [0...K-1]`, each 4 columns wide, spanning a total of 64*4=256 columns, plus a single regular decoder column in a trimmed last span at span index `k=K`.

Because we have X=3 and Y=2 in our example we have a total of X*Y=6 total loop iteratrions during training. Inference does not have a direct analogue to X because generation can, in principle, continue indefinitely; X simply captures how many autoregressive steps we choose to unroll while training. Y-looping is trivial so we only detail the three X-loops here. Let's start with x=0:

On every z=0 position we initialize the transformer state with the embedding for the input token `t` and the [|predict-next|] embedding in the "self" stream at the position `i`, and we constrain the output at position `i` to contain the embedding of the token `t+1` in the "next" stream output. That other positions z>0 receive only the [|find-self|] encoding in their "self" stream and are constrained to the token `t+z` in their "self output stream.

After Y=2 iterations we enter the `x=1` region. For the z<=x positions we (re-)add the token embedding `t+z` and [|predict-next|] to the self input stream at position i and constrain the output after Y=2 more itrations to carry the token embedding `t+z+1` in their "next" output. The z>x positions are simply left as they are (keep looping) and are again constrained to carry the token embedding of `t+z` in their output streams, and so forth.

This encoder-like intra-span coupling is intentionally stronger than what we ultimately want to ship. When X>0 we can leak ground-truth information backwards: on the second iteration (x=1) the freshly injected z=1 column can send its correct self token to z=0 via attention/beta, telling the decoder what it needs to predict. On x=2 that leakage can travel from z=2 back to z=0, then through the alpha bridge into the next span (k+1), revealing its upcoming target as well. We keep this loosened behavior for the proof-of-concept so we can verify the machinery is wired up correctly; once that is confirmed we will tighten the spec to block these retrograde channels during training.

With regards to attention a transformer with a position with k_2 and z_2 attents to all other transformers at k_1 and z_1 when either `k_1 < k_2 and z_1=0` or `k_1 == k_2`.

With regards to SANE alpha and beta channels there are two aspects. Within a goup (k_1 == k_2) there's alpha propagation from each z_1 to z_2 and beta propagation from each z_2 to z_1 where z_1+1 == z_2. From one group to the next (k_1+1 == k_2) there's alpha propagation from z_1=0 in k_1 to z_2=0 in k_2.
